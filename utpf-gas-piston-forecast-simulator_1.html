Weather logic: a weather service never draws one line for tomorrow — it runs the atmosphere model many times with slightly different starting points and reports a cone of chances. This does the same: 24 jittered copies of the market you are watching run 8 sim-hours forward. The jitter is set by the entropy — a calm, low-entropy market gets near-identical copies (a narrow, trustworthy cone); a stormy, high-entropy market gets wild copies (a wide cone, low confidence). Read the chips like a forecast: median change, the 80% cone, and P(up). This forecasts this model's market, not a real stock.

Entropy traces — the probability, traced

Two entropies, live. Shannon H (teal) measures the recent record: how unpredictable the last minutes of price moves were. Von Neumann S (gold) measures the state itself: the entropy of the density matrix ρ built from the market's macro-states, discounted by coherence κ (particles moving as a flock hide order inside the noise). Always S ≤ H — the gap between the lines is order you cannot see in the record alone. When both lines ride high, the forecast cone widens and its grade falls.

How to read the piston

Each dot is one active order, bouncing like a gas molecule. Dots run faster when their chamber is hot (T = how scattered and urgent choices are). Pressure Π is measured from the force of dots hitting the walls and the piston — nothing is assumed — and Z = ΠV / (QT) scores each chamber against an ideal gas. If demand pressure beats supply pressure, the piston slides right: the demand chamber expands, the supply chamber is squeezed, and the price (read on the ruler above) rises. Squeezing a chamber at fixed T raises its pressure — Boyle's law — until the two sides balance again. That resting point is market clearing, drawn as physics. The gold spring behind the piston is the arbitrage anchor: it pulls the price back toward fair value.

A moving piston does work on the gas, so a fast shove briefly heats the squeezed chamber before the thermostat (news flow / market makers) settles it — watch the pressure spike, then relax. A market is not a gas: people read the news; the thermostat and the fair-value spring stand for that.
